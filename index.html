About Chronoscope

Read the traces.

Chronoscope is a historical map game about reading the traces left inside old images. Each round asks a simple question: where does this image show, and when?

Observation

The answer lies not in memorization, but in observation: architecture, clothing, roads, signs, terrain, technology, and the quiet details of daily life.

Public History

The project is built as an experiment in public history, visual reasoning, and geographical imagination. Submissions require verification before they can enter the game.

Read how Chronoscope verifies dates, locations, provenance, and reuse rights.

Editorial method

How the record is made.

Chronoscope treats every playable image as both a challenge and an archival claim. Publication requires evidence for what the image shows, where it belongs on the map, when it was made or depicts, and how it may be displayed.

01

Place

Scored cases use a defensible point: a camera position, event site, named landmark, or short street segment. A city or region alone is not treated as precise evidence. For representational art, the pin marks the documented subject rather than an invented viewpoint.

02

Time

The answer year follows the most relevant documented date: exposure, depicted event, or artwork creation. Catalogue uncertainty remains visible in the displayed date range; Chronoscope does not silently turn an approximate or disputed date into certainty.

03

Sources

Museum, library, archive, monument, and government records are preferred. Secondary sources may corroborate coordinates or context, but should not replace the originating collection record when one is available.

04

Rights

Every record carries a rights or reuse note. Public-domain and open licences are preferred; attribution and source links remain attached to the record even when the image itself is freely reusable.

05

Review

Community and research-assistant proposals enter a private queue. Nothing becomes playable automatically: the curator may edit, reject, or publish only after checking image identity, place, date, context, and rights.

06

Uncertainty & Corrections

Hoaxes, contested claims, and uncertain identifications are labelled as such rather than presented as settled fact. Better evidence may lead to a revised pin, date, title, or historical record.

Chronoscope is an evolving public-history project. A published record is a documented reading of the evidence, not a claim that historical knowledge can never change.
